## 1. WEST POKOT COUNTY

### Main Crops & Hectares
| Crop | Estimated Hectares | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Maize | ~30,000 | Staple food, grown mainly in West Pokot Sub-County |
| Sorghum | ~5,000 | Low allocation (0.3-0.8 acres per farmer) |
| Finger millet | ~4,000 | Traditional crop, highlands |
| Beans | ~8,000 | Intercropped with maize |
| Irish potatoes | ~2,000 | Highland zones |
| Coffee | ~500 | Small-scale, highland zones |
| Pyrethrum | ~200 | Highland niche crop |
| Mangoes/fruits | ~1,000 | Lowland areas |

### Crop Yields
- Maize: ~1.2-1.8 t/ha (below national average due to erratic rainfall)
- Sorghum: ~0.5-0.8 t/ha
- Beans: ~0.3-0.5 t/ha
- Finger millet: ~0.4-0.7 t/ha

### Soil Type & pH
- **Highlands**: Nitisols and Cambisols (volcanic origin), pH 5.5-6.5
- **Lowlands/Valley**: Solonetz and Vertisols, pH 6.5-8.5 (saline in some areas)
- **Mid-altitude**: Sandy loams and clay loams

### Annual Rainfall
- Highlands (Cherangani): 1,200-1,600 mm
- Mid-altitude: 800-1,200 mm
- Lowlands (Pokot North): 400-600 mm

### Drought Risk Level: **2 (High)**
- ASAL county; NDMA classifies as "Normal" but frequently trends to "Alert"
- Historically drought-prone, especially in pastoral lowlands

### Irrigation Percentage: **~1.4%** of households use irrigation

### Food Security IPC Phase
- **Current (2025)**: IPC Phase 2 (Stressed)
- Pastoral zones can escalate to Phase 3 during drought
- County supported 161,112 farmers with subsidized maize seeds

### Key Agricultural Projects
- Wei Wei Irrigation Scheme
- Seker irrigation project
- County maize seed subsidy program (13,426 bales distributed)
- West Pokot County Agro-Ecology Policy 2025

### Agro-dealers: ~80-120 estimated (concentrated in Kapenguria and Makutano)

### NCPB Depot: **Kapenguria Depot** (served via Kitale regional depot)

### Livestock
- Cattle: ~600,000 (mainly Zebu/Sahiwal crosses; dairy breeds in highlands)
- Goats: ~800,000+ (meat goats, Galla goats)
- Sheep: ~400,000 (Merino crosses in highlands)
- Camels: ~15,000 (Pokot North)
- Bee-keeping significant in lowlands
- Livestock contributes ~60% of household income in pastoral zones

### Export Crops
- Limited; some mangoes and honey for regional markets

### Crop Yields
- Maize: ~1.5-2.5 t/ha (higher in irrigated Perkerra)

- Coffee: ~0.3-0.5 t/ha (Arabica)
- Beans: ~0.4-0.6 t/ha
- Sorghum: ~0.6-0.8 t/ha

### Soil Type & pH
- **Highlands (Eldama Ravine)**: Nitisols, pH 5.5-6.5
- **Lake Baringo basin**: Saline-alkaline Solonetz, pH 7.5-9.0
- **Kerio Valley**: Vertisols and Cambisols, pH 6.0-7.5
- Soil salinity is a documented problem around Lake Baringo

### Annual Rainfall
- Highlands: 1,000-1,500 mm
- Lowlands (Marigat): 600-900 mm
- Bimodal: March-May (long rains), October-December (short rains)

- NDMA classification: "Normal" but frequently "Alert" phase
- History of massive livestock losses due to rainfall variability

- Perkerra Irrigation Scheme: 2,750 acres (1,625 households)
- 26 functional irrigation schemes

### Food Security IPC Phase
- **Current (2025)**: IPC Phase 2 (Stressed); pastoral zones Phase 3 (Crisis)
- ~179,000 people in Phase 4 (Emergency) in arid parts (July-Sep 2025)

### Key Agricultural Projects
- **Perkerra Irrigation Scheme** (seed maize, green grams, rice)
- **Radat Dam** (flagship irrigation dam project)
- Coffee revitalization program
- Contract farming with Kenya Seed Company
- County Strategic Plan 2023-2027 for Agriculture

### Agro-dealers: ~100-150 estimated

### NCPB Depots: **Marigat Depot, Kabarnet Depot**

### Livestock
- Cattle: ~700,000 (Zebu predominant; dairy in highlands)
- Goats: ~1,200,000
- Sheep: ~600,000
- Camels: ~20,000 (Tiaty/East Pokot)
- Beekeeping: significant (Baringo honey)

### Export Crops
- Coffee (Arabica), honey
- Seed maize (contracted by Kenya Seed Company)

## 4. VIHIGA COUNTY

### Main Crops & Hectares
| Crop | Estimated Hectares | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Maize | ~10,000 | Staple food (26,865 acres total food crops) |
| Beans | ~5,000 | Major food crop |
| Sorghum | ~2,000 | Traditional cereal |
| Finger millet | ~1,500 | Traditional cereal |
| Tea | ~8,000 | Major cash crop (25,815 acres total cash crops) |
| Bananas | ~3,000 | Food and cash |
| Groundnuts | ~1,000 | Food crop |
| Sweet potatoes | ~1,500 | Food crop |
| Kales/vegetables | ~2,000 | Horticultural |

### Crop Yields
- Maize: ~1.6 t/ha (17.5 bags/acre, declining trend)
- Tea: ~6-8 t/ha green leaf
- Beans: ~0.4-0.6 t/ha
- Bananas: ~15-20 t/ha

### Soil Type & pH
- **Dominant**: Acrisols, Ferralsols, Nitisols
- pH: 4.5-5.5 (acidic; soil acidity is a major concern)
- Highly weathered, leached soils with iron/aluminum oxide accumulation
- Declining fertility from continuous cultivation

### Annual Rainfall: **1,900 mm** (average)
- Well distributed throughout the year
- Long rains: March-May; Short rains: October-November
- One of the wettest counties in Kenya

### Drought Risk Level: **0 (Low/Minimal)**
- High rainfall; not ASAL
- Main risk is soil degradation, not drought

### Irrigation Percentage: **<2%**
- Mostly rainfed agriculture
- Kitchen garden irrigation using spring water

### Food Security IPC Phase
- **Current**: IPC Phase 1-2 (Minimal to Stressed)
- Not typically included in ASAL IPC analysis
- Agriculture provides 70% of employment, 34% of county economy

### Key Agricultural Projects
- Agroecology Policy development
- Agroforestry technologies promotion
- Tea factory improvements (KTDA)
- Soil fertility restoration programs
- County Climate-Smart Agriculture initiatives

### Agro-dealers: ~60-100 estimated (small county, high density)

### NCPB Depot: **Chavakali Depot**

### Livestock
- Dairy cattle: ~50,000 (zero-grazing predominant due to small farm sizes)
- Poultry: significant (indigenous and layers)
- Goats: ~30,000
- Average farm size: 0.4 ha (one of smallest in Kenya)

### Export Crops
- **Tea** (primary through KTDA)
- Limited other exports due to small farm sizes

### Crop Yields
- Maize: ~1.0-1.5 t/ha (mean production ~100,167 tonnes over 11 years)
- Sorghum: ~0.94 t/ha (performs better than maize in low rainfall)
- Beans: ~0.4-0.6 t/ha
- Rice: ~3.0-4.0 t/ha

### Soil Type & pH
- **Dominant**: Planosols, Vertisols (Kano plains area), Ferralsols
- pH: 5.0-6.0 (acidic; liming recommended)
- Black cotton soils in low-lying areas

### Annual Rainfall: **1,000-2,000 mm**
- Trimodal: Long rains (March-May), continental rains (June-August), short rains (October-December)
- Relatively reliable but seasonal failures increasing

### Drought Risk Level: **1 (Moderate)**
- Not ASAL but food deficit county
- Strong negative correlation (-0.540) between drought and crop production

### Irrigation Percentage: **<3%**
- Kitchen-garden scale using spring water and trenches
- **Lower Nzoia Irrigation Project** (shared with Busia, 20,000 acres planned)
- Dominion Farms (rice, abandoned)

### Food Security IPC Phase
- **Current**: IPC Phase 2 (Stressed)
- Food deficit county; meets food needs for only ~4 months/year
- Poverty rate: 47.56%
- Agriculture provides 60% household income, 61% employment

### Key Agricultural Projects
- Kenya Agricultural Carbon Project (KACP) - Vi Agroforestry
- Lower Nzoia Irrigation Project
- Rice value chain development
- Conservation agriculture promotion
- Sorghum/millet improvement programs

### Agro-dealers: ~120-160 estimated

### NCPB Depot: **Bondo Depot**

### Livestock
- Cattle: ~300,000 (indigenous, with some dairy improvement)
- Goats: ~150,000
- Sheep: ~50,000
- Poultry: significant
- **Fisheries**: major (Lake Victoria; Bondo fish landing sites)

### Export Crops
- Limited; some regional trade in sorghum and cassava products

## DATA SOURCES AND NOTES

### Reliability Notes
- Hectare figures are estimates compiled from multiple sources (CIDPs, KNBS reports, sector reports) and may vary by year
- Crop yields represent typical ranges; actual yields vary significantly by season and locality
- Agro-dealer numbers are estimates based on national surveys extrapolated to county level
- IPC phases are from the most recent FEWS NET/IPC analysis (July 2025 - January 2026)
- NCPB depot assignments are approximate; some counties are served by depots in neighboring counties
- Soil types are generalized from Kenya Soil Survey data; significant local variation exists

### Key Data Gaps
- Precise county-level hectarage data for individual crops (requires access to full KNBS National Agriculture Production Reports)
- Exact agro-dealer counts per county (national mapping ongoing)
- Current livestock census data (last comprehensive census may be outdated)
- Precise irrigation percentages (many small-scale irrigation not captured)
